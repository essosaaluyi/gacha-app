# R1 Triplets Baby Dragon

## Sonic Ultimate User Storyboard - Production Direction v2

Status: current creative authority for the R1 fatal-mode player cutscene.

This version replaces the previous nest-launch attack progression. The player cutscene contains only the R1 trio and the moving sonic pressure effect. Enemy block, survive, hit, and fall footage remains a separate interchangeable scene.

## Creative Lock

- Exactly three baby dragons whenever characters are visible.
- The three dragons retain the identity, proportions, markings, crystals, fins, limbs, and tails from the R1 character master reference.
- Opening hook: three faces fill the entire frame and are cropped by the frame edges.
- Their eyes open with comic snap timing, but the resulting energy feels dangerous and premium.
- The preparation stage is featureless black with a soft translucent gray circular light pool. There is no visible spotlight cone.
- Lightning appears behind the trio and creates a backlit silhouette edge. It never strikes a character.
- The inhale is shown from an upper-front angle. Cheeks become round and cute while the skull, snout, eyes, and crystal geometry remain stable.
- The maximum inhale is held for a full 24 frames while the bodies perform a small charged wiggle.
- The release travels from lower left to upper right as the camera pulls away.
- The release background changes to abstract blue anime streams. No realistic ground or environment appears.
- The sonic attack is moving compressed-air distortion, not fire, a laser, or a solid projectile.
- The attack-travel scene contains no enemy.
- The final victory insert is a separate optional clip for result-aware assembly.

## Delivery Format

- Master format: 16:9, 1920x1080 delivery, 24 fps.
- Working edit: 15 seconds, 360 frames.
- Generate four independent source clips and assemble them in edit.
- Keep at least 8 clean frames before and after every usable action for trimming.
- Use hard cuts, graphic impact frames, or motivated VFX coverage. Do not cross-dissolve character poses.

## Scene A - Eye Ignition

Storyboard sheet: `r1-ekonte-scene-a-eye-ignition-v2.png`

### A01 | F000-F011 | 12f | extreme close-up | 100mm

Picture: Three dragon heads fill the frame. The center face is slightly forward. The left and right faces are deliberately cut by the side edges, but all three closed eye lines and all three forehead crystals remain readable.

Camera: Fixed. No idle drift.

Stage: Nearly black. Only a weak soft gray circle separates their silhouettes from the background.

### A02 | F012-F023 | 12f | eye snap with half-second push-in | 100mm to 115mm equivalent

Action: All six eyes spring open on the same beat. Use one overshoot drawing with pupils comically small, then settle to the normal amber-eye design within three frames. Every pupil resolves directly toward the camera.

Camera: Push/zoom toward the three faces continuously for the full 12 frames, ending closer than A01. Keep the move centered on the middle dragon without cropping away either side dragon.

FX: Six tiny white-cyan ignition sparks appear at the eyelid corners. No eye beams.

Sound: Tight six-note sparkle compressed into one beat, followed by a bass tick.

### A03 | F024-F035 | 12f | charged direct-to-camera close-up | 115mm

Action: All six pupils remain locked directly into the camera. Heads face front without looking upper right. Mouths stay closed. Ear fins give one small outward twitch.

FX: Thin hand-drawn ripple contours expand from each eye pair, overlap briefly, and disappear before reaching the frame border. Use irregular open arcs, not perfect circles or glass rings.

### A04 | F036-F043 | 8f | super-fast pull-out | 115mm to 40mm equivalent

Camera: Pull straight backward from extreme close-up to mid-shot in eight frames with strong depth parallax. Use at most one distortion drawing and one recovery drawing. Do not create a long zoom blur.

Action: The trio holds its stare while the frame expands around them.

### A05 | F044-F057 | 14f | mid-shot lightning reveal | 40mm

Picture: Exactly three full upper bodies in a compact triangular formation. All three heads and all six pupils face directly into the camera. Two or three lightning strokes drop vertically behind them at different depths.

Lighting: Backlight rises for two frames and creates hard cyan-white rim shapes on crystals, fins, shoulders, and cheek edges. Front faces stay readable.

### A06 | F058-F071 | 14f | charged mid-shot hold | 40mm

Picture: The trio continues staring directly into the camera as the lightning afterglow fades to a controlled silhouette rim. The gray circular light pool remains soft and translucent. No visible cone, horizon, floor texture, debris, smoke, or scenery.

Transition: Hard cut on a final residual lightning flicker to the upper-angle inhale shot.

## Scene B - Inhale And Release

Storyboard sheet: `r1-ekonte-scene-b-inhale-release-v2.png`

### B01 | F072-F091 | 20f | upper-front angle | 50mm

Picture: Camera looks down approximately 25 degrees. The trio forms a compact triangle with the center dragon slightly rearward. All three faces and front paws remain unobstructed.

Action: Bodies lower, paws brace, nostrils narrow, and mouths seal. The inhale begins visibly through the throat and lower cheeks.

### B02 | F092-F115 | 24f | inhale expansion | 65mm

Camera: Slow controlled push toward the three faces.

Action: Cheeks expand continuously into cute round puffs. Expansion remains attached to the jaw and does not alter eye spacing, snout length, forehead crystals, or overall head width. Chests rise and stomachs tuck inward.

FX: Fine cyan suction strokes travel from the frame edges toward the trio. No particles enter the mouths.

### B03 | F116-F139 | 24f | one-second charge hold | 65mm

Camera: Perform a continuous teasing push-pull zoom for the entire 24-frame hold. Pulse quickly outward, inward, outward, then inward with a restrained 4-6 percent framing change. Keep the optical center fixed between the three heads and preserve the upper-front angle. Each reversal eases for one frame so the motion reads as intentional camera teasing rather than digital jitter.

Action: Cheek volume stays constant. The trio performs a small rhythmic body wiggle caused by contained pressure: left, right, center, then settle. The body wiggle opposes the camera pulse slightly for elastic energy. Feet never slide and spacing never changes.

FX: Crystals pulse once in sequence left-center-right. Fine line vibration stays behind the silhouette.

Sound: Air-pressure whine climbs while three muffled squeaks create a playful edge.

### B04 | F140-F147 | 8f | final teasing zoom and release threshold | 65mm

Camera: Continue the same push-pull rhythm with one final quick zoom out and snap back in. Land at the closest framing on F146. Frame F147 breaks the pattern with a single black-white-cyan graphic impact drawing.

Action: The body wiggle continues through the final camera pulse, then stops sharply on F146. Pupils sharpen and bodies compress one last time.

Background: The gray stage circle collapses toward the trio, preparing the blue stream transition.

### B05 | F148-F177 | 30f | diagonal release | 65mm to 24mm equivalent

Camera: Explosive pull-out and slight rise. The dragons resolve into the lower-left corner while the attack path opens toward the upper-right corner.

Action: Mouths open together. Cheeks deflate over four to six frames. Forelegs lock and bodies recoil down-left while ear fins and tails lag by two frames.

FX: One shared compressed-air front launches diagonally upper right. Show it with broken white pressure edges, cyan refraction cuts, and a narrow transparent distortion band. Do not create three beams.

### B06 | F178-F215 | 38f | ultra-wide source handoff | 24mm

Picture: The trio remains small at lower left. The moving pressure front exits toward upper right. Blue-cyan anime streams replace the black preparation stage and establish the travel direction.

Camera: Pull-out eases to a stop while the attack keeps accelerating, making the pressure front visibly outrun the camera.

Transition: Cut on the pressure front crossing the upper-right edge. Match its direction and line velocity into Scene C.

## Scene C - Sonic Travel

Storyboard sheet: `r1-ekonte-scene-c-sonic-travel-v1.png`

### C01 | F216-F229 | 14f | origin pickup | 28mm

Picture: Begin near the dragons at lower left and catch the rear edge of the pressure front already moving away toward upper right. The trio quickly falls behind and leaves frame.

Camera: Accelerate along the attack path. Never pass through a dragon.

### C02 | F230-F247 | 18f | three-quarter rear tracking | 35mm

Camera: Follow just behind and below the moving distortion. Blue stream layers move at three different speeds, creating clear parallax.

FX: The sonic front has no solid center. It is readable only through displaced background lines, broken condensation slashes, and brief white-cyan edge accents.

### C03 | F248-F265 | 18f | parallel side tracking | 50mm

Camera: Draw level with the pressure front and track beside it. The front translates diagonally while the stream field races in the opposite relative direction.

Action physics: Use short two-to-five-frame line fragments. No long colored afterimages.

### C04 | F266-F279 | 14f | camera moves ahead | 65mm

Camera: Accelerate past the pressure front, bank gently, and look back down its path. The movement remains physically continuous and uses the blue line field as motivated coverage.

### C05 | F280-F291 | 12f | incoming pressure | 70mm

Picture: The front approaches as an open, broken diagonal distortion boundary that continues beyond the frame. It grows by translation and scale, never by becoming a sphere, ring, dome, tunnel, or glass wall.

### C06 | F292-F299 | 8f | lens overtake and exit | graphic impact

FX: The pressure front overtakes the lens with one asymmetric black-white-cyan impact drawing. Residual blue streams continue upper right.

Transition: End on clean moving abstract lines. This is the editorial handoff for an interchangeable enemy block, survive, hit, or fall clip.

## Performance Risks

- Six simultaneous eye opens can drift or duplicate without a strict three-character reference.
- Extreme face crops make extra heads and fused eyes more likely in generated video.
- Rounded cheeks can become glossy spheres or alter skull proportions; use the storyboard pose and master sheet together.
- Lightning can be mistaken for an attack beam if it overlaps mouths or faces.
- A diagonal sonic effect may collapse into a beam, ring, bubble, or tunnel without explicit open-boundary rules.
- A tracking camera can cause the attack to reverse direction or teleport; preserve lower-left to upper-right screen direction in every cut.
- The victory insert can imply a win too early; keep it optional and result-controlled.
